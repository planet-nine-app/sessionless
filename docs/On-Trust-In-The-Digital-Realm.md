# On Trust In The Digital Realm

Recently there has been interest in the use of Sessionless for providing digital signatures to documents on the web. 
This is an exciting use case for this humble protocol, and one that gives me occasional anxiety.
So I figured it would be good to discuss the implications of the use of Sessionless for establishing trust in the context of signing agreements of any kind.

## What is trust?

To a certain extent we can just ignore this question. 
If you had trust in the person or people you're looking to sign agreements with, you wouldn't need the agreement. 
It's the lack of trust, which requires you to write stuff down. 

The subtext of this is that the agreement will somehow be enforceable should the agreement, and the trust it represents, be broken. 
The daytime judge shows I watched while home sick suggest that in the US at least there is a medium for resolution of these breeches. 
But when I think of a globally available signature system, I consider that not every nation is willing to pay out lucrative television contracts to provide for agreement adjudication, and so I just want people to be clear on what a signature is. 

### Who is trusting who?

So let's get down to the business. 
When something is signed digitally, unless you personally see it happening, there is no way of being certain of who did the signing. 

This is because in the digital realm, who you are is established by one of these three things:

* Something you know (a password)
* Something you have (a key)
* Something you are (thumbprint)

All of these can be shared, copied, stolen, lost, or, in the case of the last one, gruesomely removed. 
No matter what convoluted scheme is devised in an attempt to gaurantee someone is who they say they are it can be exploited, and the more valuable it is to exploit, the more of a target it becomes.
